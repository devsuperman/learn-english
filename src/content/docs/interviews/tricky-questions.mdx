## Explanation

After *"Tell me about yourself"*, recruiters ask a few questions that seem simple but are easy to answer badly. The rule for all of them: **be honest, be positive, and connect your answer to this job.** Keep each answer to **30–60 seconds**.

### 1. "Why are you looking for a new job?"

Talk about what you're moving **toward**, not what you're running **away from**. Never criticize your current company or manager.

> *I've learned a lot at my current company, especially about payments. Now I'm looking for a bigger technical challenge and the chance to work in an international team, and this role offers both.*

### 2. "Why do you want to work here?"

Show you did your research. Mention something **specific**: the product, the tech stack, the engineering blog, the mission.

> *I've been using your app for two years, and I read your engineering blog post about migrating to Kotlin. I'd love to work on a product at that scale, with a team that writes about what they learn.*

### 3. "What are your strengths?"

Choose **one or two** strengths that matter for the role, and give a short example. A list of adjectives (*"I'm proactive, hard-working, a team player"*) sounds empty.

> *One of my strengths is debugging complex problems. Last year I found the cause of a memory leak that had been affecting us for months, by profiling the service in production.*

### 4. "What is your biggest weakness?"

Choose a **real** weakness that is **not essential** for the job, and show what you're doing to improve. Avoid fake weaknesses like *"I'm a perfectionist."*

> *I used to find it hard to say no to extra tasks, and sometimes I got overloaded. Now I make my priorities visible to my manager, and we decide together what to drop.*

> *Honestly, my spoken English is still improving. I practice every day, and I already work with English documentation and write all my code reviews in English.*

### 5. "Where do you see yourself in five years?"

Show ambition that fits the company. You don't need a precise plan.

> *I'd like to grow into a senior or staff role, where I can make architecture decisions and mentor other engineers.*

### 6. "Do you have any gaps in your résumé?"

Be short and honest, then move back to your skills.

> *Yes, I took six months off to care for a family member. During that time I also did an AWS certification, and I'm ready to get back to full-time work.*

## Key phrases

- *I'm looking for a role where I can…*
- *What attracted me to this position is…*
- *One thing I really like about your company is…*
- *One of my strengths is… For example, …*
- *Something I'm working on is… What I'm doing about it is…*
- *In the long term, I'd like to…*

## Common mistakes

| ❌ Don't say | ✅ Say | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| My boss is terrible. | I'm looking for a team with more focus on code quality. | Never criticize your employer. |
| I want to earn more money. | I'm looking for the next step in my career. | Money is a valid reason, but talk about it in the salary conversation. |
| I'm a perfectionist. | *(a real weakness + what you do about it)* | Recruiters hear this cliché every day. |
| I don't have weaknesses. | *Something I'm working on is…* | It sounds arrogant or unprepared. |
| I want to work here because it's a big company. | *(something specific about them)* | It could be any company. |
