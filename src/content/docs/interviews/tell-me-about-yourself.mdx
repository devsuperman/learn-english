## Explanation

This is almost always the **first question**. It is not about your life story. It is a **60–90 second pitch** that connects your experience to the job.

Use the **Present → Past → Future** structure:

1. **Present**: your current role and what you do.
2. **Past**: one or two experiences or achievements that are relevant to this job.
3. **Future**: why you want *this* job.

### Example (backend developer)

> I'm a backend developer with five years of experience, and I currently work at a fintech company, where I build payment APIs in Java and Spring.
>
> Before that, I worked at an e-commerce startup, where I led the migration from a monolith to microservices. That cut our deployment time from two hours to fifteen minutes.
>
> Now I'm looking for a role where I can work on large-scale distributed systems, and that's why I'm excited about this position.

## Key phrases

- *I'm a **[role]** with **[X] years of experience** in **[area]**.*
- *I **currently** work at **[company]**, where I **[main responsibility]**.*
- *Before that, I **worked at / was** …*
- *One thing I'm proud of is …*
- *I'm looking for a role where I can …*
- *That's why I'm excited about this position.*

## Common mistakes

| ❌ Don't say | ✅ Say | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I **have** 28 years. | I'm 28. *(or don't mention age)* | Age uses *be*. Usually, leave it out. |
| I **work** here **since** 2021. | I've **worked** here since 2021. | *Since* + a point in time takes the present perfect. |
| I **am** developer. | I'm **a** developer. | Jobs need *a/an*. |
| I'm **graduated in** computer science. | I **have a degree in** computer science. | More natural phrasing. |
| I'm looking for **new challenges**. | I'm looking for a role where I can **[specific goal]**. | Be specific. "New challenges" sounds generic. |

:::tip[Grammar for this lesson]
This answer mixes the past simple (*I worked*) and the present perfect (*I've worked*). Review [Past simple vs. present perfect](../../grammar/past-vs-present-perfect/) if you're not sure which to use.
:::

1. Write your answer with the Present → Past → Future structure.
