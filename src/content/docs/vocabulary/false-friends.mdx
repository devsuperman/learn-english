## Explanation

**False friends** are English words that look like words in your language but have a **different meaning**. They are common for speakers of Portuguese, Spanish, French and Italian, but speakers of many languages mix them up. They are dangerous because you feel confident when you use them, so nobody realizes the misunderstanding.

## False friends at work

| English word | What it really means | What you probably mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **actually** | in fact, really | **currently**, at the moment |
| **eventually** | in the end, after a long time | **occasionally**, sometimes / **possibly** |
| **pretend** | to act as if something is true (fake) | **intend**, **plan** to |
| **attend** | to go to (a meeting, a course) | **assist**, **help**, **serve** (a customer) |
| **assist** | to help | **attend**, be present at |
| **realize** | to understand suddenly | **do**, **carry out**, **hold** (an event) |
| **resume** (verb) | to continue after a pause | **summarize** |
| **support** | to help, to back | **stand**, **tolerate**, **bear** |
| **library** | a place to borrow books / a code library | **bookstore** (a shop) |
| **college** | university (US) | high school |
| **parents** | mother and father only | **relatives** (all your family) |
| **sensible** | reasonable, practical | **sensitive** (easily affected) |
| **exit** | a way out | **success** |
| **lecture** | a talk, a class | **reading** |
| **push / pull** | push = away from you, pull = toward you | (on doors, often confused) |
| **notice** | to see / an announcement | **news** |
| **costume** | clothes for a party or a play | **habit**, **custom** |

### In tech, specifically

- A **library** in code (*a React library*) is correct, but a shop that sells books is a **bookstore**.
- **Résumé** (noun, US) / **CV** (UK) = the document you send for a job. **To resume** (verb) = to continue. *Let's resume the meeting after lunch.*
- **Deception** = lying to someone. When you feel let down, it's **disappointment**.

## Confusing pairs (for everyone)

| Pair | Difference | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **lose / loose** | *lose* = not have anymore; *loose* = not tight | *Don't **lose** your API key. / a **loose** connection* |
| **affect / effect** | *affect* = verb; *effect* = noun | *The bug **affects** users. / It has no **effect**.* |
| **its / it's** | *its* = possessive; *it's* = it is | ***It's** fast because of **its** cache.* |
| **borrow / lend** | *borrow* = take; *lend* = give | *Can I **borrow** your charger? / Can you **lend** me…?* |
| **advice / advise** | *advice* = noun (uncountable); *advise* = verb | *Can you give me some **advice**? / I'd **advise** you to…* |
| **then / than** | *then* = after that; *than* = comparison | *Faster **than** before. First test, **then** deploy.* |
| **fun / funny** | *fun* = enjoyable; *funny* = makes you laugh | *The hackathon was **fun**. The meme was **funny**.* |

## Exercises

<Quiz
  question="You want to say you work at a bank now. Which is correct?"
  options={["Actually, I work at a bank.", "Currently, I work at a bank.", "Eventually, I work at a bank."]}
  answer={1}
  explanation="'Currently' means 'now'. 'Actually' means 'in fact', and 'eventually' means 'in the end'."
/>

<Quiz
  question="'I ___ to learn Rust next year.' (it's my plan)"
  options={["pretend", "intend", "attend"]}
  answer={1}
  explanation="'Intend' means 'plan to'. 'Pretend' means to fake something."
/>

<Quiz
  question="'Can you ___ me some advice about the interview?'"
  options={["borrow", "give", "advise"]}
  answer={1}
  explanation="You 'give' advice. You can also say 'Can you advise me…', but not 'advise me some advice'."
/>

<FixIt
  sentence="I assisted the meeting yesterday."
  answers={["I attended the meeting yesterday.", "I went to the meeting yesterday."]}
  explanation="'Attend' means 'go to'. 'Assist' means 'help'."
/>

<FixIt
  sentence="We will realize the deploy on Friday."
  answers={["We will do the deploy on Friday.", "We will deploy on Friday.", "We'll deploy on Friday.", "We'll do the deploy on Friday.", "We will do the deployment on Friday."]}
  explanation="'Realize' means 'understand'. For actions, use 'do', or the verb itself: deploy."
/>

<FixIt
  sentence="The new feature was a big exit."
  answers={["The new feature was a big success.", "The new feature was a great success."]}
  explanation="'Exit' is a way out. The word you want is 'success'."
/>
