## Explanation

| | Present simple | Present continuous |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Use | Routines, facts, permanent situations | Actions happening now, or temporary situations |
| Form | *I work / she works* | *I'm working / she's working* |
| Signal words | usually, every day, always, often | now, currently, this week, at the moment |
| Tech example | *I **work** on the payments team.* | *This sprint I'**m working** on the checkout redesign.* |

Remember the **-s** for *he / she / it* in the present simple: *The service **runs** on AWS. She **leads** the team.*

### Verbs that don't use the continuous

Some verbs describe states, not actions. Use them in the simple form: **know, understand, need, want, like, believe, mean, depend**.

- ✅ *I **understand** the problem.* ❌ *I'm understanding the problem.*
- ✅ *It **depends** on the load.* ❌ *It's depending on the load.*

## Key phrases

- *I'm a frontend developer. I **build** user interfaces with React.*
- *Our team **deploys** to production twice a week.*
- *Right now I'**m migrating** our tests to Playwright.*
- *We'**re hiring** two more engineers this quarter.*
- *The service **handles** about 10,000 requests per second.*

## Common mistakes

| ❌ Don't say | ✅ Say | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| She **work** on the backend. | She **works** on the backend. | *he/she/it* takes **-s**. |
| I **am work** at a startup. | I **work** at a startup. / I'**m working** at a startup. | Don't mix *am* + base verb. |
| I'm **needing** access to the repo. | I **need** access to the repo. | *Need* is a state verb. |
| Now I **develop** a new feature. | Right now I'**m developing** a new feature. | Actions happening now use the continuous. |
