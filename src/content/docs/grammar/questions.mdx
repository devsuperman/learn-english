## Explanation

In many languages, you make a question just by changing your intonation: *"You use Docker?"* In English, most questions need a **helper verb** (an auxiliary) **before the subject**.

| Main verb | Helper | Structure | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Present simple | **do / does** | do/does + subject + base verb | ***Do** you **use** Docker?* |
| Past simple | **did** | did + subject + base verb | ***Did** you **deploy** it?* |
| *be* | none | be + subject | ***Is** the build green?* |
| Present perfect | **have / has** | have/has + subject + past participle | ***Have** you **tested** it?* |
| Modals | **can, could, will, should** | modal + subject + base verb | ***Can** you **share** your screen?* |

After **do / does / did**, the main verb is always in the **base form**: *Did you **fix** it?* (not *fixed*). *Does she **work** remotely?* (not *works*).

### Wh- questions

Put the question word first, then the same structure:

- ***What** do you **do**?*
- ***Where** did you **find** the bug?*
- ***How long** have you **worked** with React?*
- ***Why** is the pipeline **failing**?*

## Key phrases

- *Do you have any questions for us?*
- *Did you have a chance to look at my PR?*
- *How does the authentication work?*
- *What did you change in the config?*
- *Have you worked with microservices before?*

## Common mistakes

| ❌ Don't say | ✅ Say | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You **use** Docker? | **Do** you **use** Docker? | Questions need *do/does/did*. |
| **Does** he **works** remotely? | **Does** he **work** remotely? | After *does*, use the base verb. |
| **Did** you **fixed** the bug? | **Did** you **fix** the bug? | After *did*, use the base verb. |
| What **means** "SLA"? | What **does** "SLA" **mean**? | *Does* + base verb. |
| **Do** you **are** ready? | **Are** you ready? | With *be*, don't use *do*. |
| Why the test **is** failing? | Why **is** the test failing? | Helper verb **before** the subject. |
