## Explanation

Use **do** to make questions in the **present simple** (habits, facts, things that are true in general) when the subject is:

- **I, you, we, they**
- a **plural noun**: *the tests, the developers, your teammates, the servers*

| Question word | Helper | Subject | Base verb | Rest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| | **Do** | you | **use** | TypeScript? |
| | **Do** | the tests | **run** | on every commit? |
| **How often** | **do** | you | **deploy**? | |
| **How** | **do** | I | **run** | the project locally? |

The main verb stays in the **base form**: no *-s*, no *-ed*.

*Do* can also be the main verb. In *What **do** you **do**?*, the first *do* is the helper and the second *do* is the main verb. The question means *"What is your job?"*

**Short answers:** *Yes, I do.* / *No, I don't.* / *Yes, they do.*

:::caution[When you don't need do]
With **be** (*am, are*) and with **modals** (*can, could, will, should*), don't use *do*: *"**Are** you ready?"*, *"**Can** you share your screen?"*
:::

## Key phrases

- *Do you use TypeScript at work?*
- *Do we have a standup today?*
- *Do they work remotely?*
- *Do the tests run on every commit?*
- *How do I run the project locally?*
- *What do you do?*
- *How often do you deploy?*

## Common mistakes

| ❌ Don't say | ✅ Say | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You **use** Docker? | **Do** you **use** Docker? | A question needs *do* before the subject. |
| **Does** you work remotely? | **Do** you work remotely? | *Does* is only for *he, she, it*. |
| Do the tests **passes**? | Do the tests **pass**? | After *do*, use the base verb. |
| **Do** you **are** on call this week? | **Are** you on call this week? | With *be*, don't use *do*. |
| How I **run** the tests? | How **do** I **run** the tests? | Question word + *do* + subject + verb. |
| **Do** you **can** share your screen? | **Can** you share your screen? | With *can*, don't use *do*. |

## Today's task

A new developer joins your team tomorrow. **Write five questions with *do*** to get to know them: three yes/no questions and two wh- questions. For example: *"Do you prefer frontend or backend?"*, *"Which editor do you use?"*

Then **say them out loud**. Check that *do* comes **before** the subject every time.
