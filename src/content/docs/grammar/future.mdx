## Explanation

English has three common ways to talk about the future.

| Form | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **going to** + verb | A plan you **already decided** | *Today I'**m going to** write the tests.* |
| **will** + verb | A decision you make **now**, a promise, an offer, a prediction | *I'**ll** take a look after lunch.* |
| **present continuous** | An arrangement with a fixed time | *We'**re releasing** on Friday.* |

In standups, **going to** is the most natural form for "today's plan". When someone asks for help and you offer on the spot, use **will**: *"Sure, I'll check it."*

### Estimates and predictions

- *It'**ll probably take** two days.*
- *I **don't think** it'**ll be** ready by Friday.* (more natural than *I think it won't be…*)
- *It **should be** done by tomorrow.* (= I expect it)

## Key phrases

- *Today I'm going to pair with Maria on the API.*
- *I'll send you the link in a minute.*
- *We're having a retro on Thursday.*
- *I'll get back to you on that.* (= I'll answer later)
- *It should be ready by end of day.*

## Common mistakes

| ❌ Don't say | ✅ Say | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I **will to** fix it. | I **will fix** it. / I'll fix it. | No *to* after *will*. |
| I **go to** fix it tomorrow. | I'm **going to** fix it tomorrow. | Use *am going to*. |
| I'm going to **send** you the link now, OK? *(offer)* | I'**ll send** you the link now. | Spontaneous offers use *will*. |
| I think it **won't** be ready. | I **don't think** it'll be ready. | More natural in English. |
