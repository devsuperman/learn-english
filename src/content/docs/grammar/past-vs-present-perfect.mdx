## Explanation

In interviews, you talk about the past **all the time**. English has two main tenses for it.

| | Past simple | Present perfect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Use | A **finished** time or event | Experience **without** a specific time, or something that **continues until now** |
| Form | *I worked / I built* | *I've worked / I've built* |
| Signal words | yesterday, last year, in 2021, when I was at…, ago | for, since, ever, never, already, yet, so far |
| Example | *I **worked** at Nubank **from 2019 to 2022**.* | *I'**ve worked** with Python **for five years**.* |

**The key rule:** if you say *when* it happened (a finished time), use the **past simple**. If the time isn't finished, or the time doesn't matter, use the **present perfect**.

- *I **led** a migration **last year**.* → finished time
- *I'**ve led** two migrations.* → experience, no time given
- *I'**ve worked** here **since** 2021.* → started in the past, still true

## Key phrases

- *I **started** my career as a QA analyst **in 2018**.*
- *I'**ve been** a developer **for** six years.*
- *I'**ve never used** Rust, but I'**ve worked** a lot with Go.*
- *At my last job, I **built** an internal tool that **saved** 10 hours a week.*
- *Have you **ever worked** in an international team?*
- *So far, we'**ve migrated** 80% of the services.*

## Common mistakes

| ❌ Don't say | ✅ Say | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I **work** here **since** 2020. | I'**ve worked** here since 2020. | *Since* + still true = present perfect. |
| I **have worked** there **in** 2019. | I **worked** there in 2019. | A finished time = past simple. |
| I'**ve finished** it **yesterday**. | I **finished** it yesterday. | *Yesterday* = finished time. |
| I'm a developer **since** 5 years. | I've been a developer **for** 5 years. | *For* + duration, *since* + starting point. |
| I **have** 5 years **in** Java. | I **have 5 years of experience with** Java. | Correct expression. |
