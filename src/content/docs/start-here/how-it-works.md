**English for Tech** is a crash course for people who already know some English (around A2–B1) and want to **get hired and work in English** in the tech industry.

## What you will be able to do

By the end of the course you will be able to:

- Introduce yourself and describe your experience clearly.
- Answer the most common interview questions with structured, confident answers.
- Explain technical work: what you built, why, and what you learned.
- Write clear Slack messages, emails and pull request comments.
- Take part in standups, meetings and feedback conversations.

## How a lesson works

Every lesson has the same five parts and takes **15–20 minutes**:

1. **Explanation**: the idea in a few short paragraphs.
2. **Key phrases**: sentences you can copy and adapt.
3. **Common mistakes**: what learners often get wrong, and the fix.
4. **Exercises**: quizzes and "fix the sentence" practice, with instant feedback.
5. **Today's task**: one small real-world action, like writing, recording or saying something.

## Suggested schedule (4 weeks)

You can follow the modules in order, or start with the one you need most. If you have an interview soon, start with **Job interviews** and do one **Grammar** lesson per day alongside it.


| Week | Module | Goal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Grammar + Daily practice | Fix the structures you use every day and build a daily habit |
| 2 | Job interviews (part 1) | Record your "Tell me about yourself" |
| 3 | Job interviews (part 2) | Do a full mock interview |
| 4 | Vocabulary + Working in English | Use English in your real (or simulated) work |

:::tip[Record yourself on day 1]
Record a 1-minute video introducing yourself today. Record it again at the end of the course. Comparing the two is the best way to see your progress.
:::
